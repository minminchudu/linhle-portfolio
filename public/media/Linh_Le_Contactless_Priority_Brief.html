Mastercard’s global contactless share is ~70%. The U.S. sits at 19.7%. The standard read is that the gap is large and slow to close — but that’s not quite right. Visit frequency explains 94% of the variation in adoption across sectors, and three sectors sit at the steepest part of their S-curve right now. They don’t have the largest gaps. They have the most reachable barriers and the fastest return on merchant enablement spend.

Key Analytical Findings
Frequency Drives Adoption, Not Gap Size
Visit frequency explains 94% of the variation in contactless adoption across sectors. Average ticket adds nothing once frequency is controlled for (p = 0.46). The consequence is operational: ranking sectors by gap size produces almost the reverse of ranking them by frequency. Healthcare sits 55 points below benchmark and is visited twice a year; weekly categories close their gaps unaided. Same investment, opposite return.
Three Sectors Are at S-Curve Inflection Now
General Merchandise, Casual Restaurant, and Drug Store are the only cluster where every member sits within a year of its modelled inflection. Pulling enablement forward 18 months lifts the 2025 national baseline from 65% to 78%. The curves converge by 2028, so this buys timing, not a higher ceiling: the window is open now and closes on its own.
Behavioral vs. Infrastructure Barriers Differ
By 2022, 87.5% of in-person card volume already ran on chip terminals, yet only 22.5% of it was tapped (Fed NPIPS). The hardware is in the field. Atlanta Fed DCPC points to the same conclusion from the consumer side: 38% of non-users say they do not know whether the terminal accepts a tap. That is a configuration and prompting problem, not a capital deployment problem, and it is far cheaper to fix.
Structural Resistors Should Wait
Healthcare and Hardware carry large raw gaps but low frequency and high infrastructure cost. Receptivity multiplier (×0.45) reflects slower ROI. Investment here dilutes return vs. the laggard cluster.
